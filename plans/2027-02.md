# February 2027 — Stage 2, and the employability track opens

Month 5 of [the plan](../README.md). **15 h/week: 2.5 h/day, Monday to Saturday, Sunday off.**

Scope only — what to read, what to build, what has to exist by Sunday. Setup and tooling are deliberately not here. Standing rules are in [plans/README.md](README.md).

February 2027 begins on a Monday and ends on a Sunday. Four clean weeks, no holidays, sixty hours — the first month since November with nothing taken out of it.

Two things change this month. Mathematics ends, and the [employability track](../README.md#stage-2--systems) starts with the five hours it releases. From here on, five hours of every week are spent on the part of this plan that someone will hire a beginner for.

## Sources in use this month

- [Computer Networking: a Top-Down Approach](https://gaia.cs.umass.edu/kurose_ross/online_lectures.htm) — chapters 7 and 8, **from the book**. The lecture site lists both by title and has never published material for either, so there is nothing to watch. Section numbers are not given below for that reason; the topics are.
- [Nand to Tetris Part I](https://www.nand2tetris.org/course) — modules 1 and 2, projects 1 and 2. Six modules in the course; this month takes the first two.
- [Web Security Academy](https://portswigger.net/web-security) — the server-side topics, in the order the Academy itself recommends for beginners. [OWASP Top 10](https://owasp.org/www-project-top-ten/) alongside, as context rather than reading.
- [Mathematics for Computer Science](https://openlearninglibrary.mit.edu/courses/course-v1:OCW+6.042J+2T2019/about) — lesson 2.11 and problem set 8, and then it is finished.

> Why SQL injection first: it is what the Academy puts first, and its own reason is a good one — you only have to reason about the server. Everything you learned about HTTP in December applies directly, and nothing about the browser has to be held in your head yet.

## Rules for a week that goes wrong

The [standing rules](README.md) apply. Three things specific to this month:

- **The PortSwigger hours are the protected ones.** Every other month you can argue the low-level work matters more, and you would usually be right. Not here: this is the track the [month 9–10 checkpoint](../README.md#employability-checkpoint--months-910) stands on, and it has to be finished before [eJPT](../README.md#certifications) in months 6 to 7. If something gives, let it be Nand to Tetris — that course waits patiently and the calendar does not.
- **A lab you read the solution for is not a lab you solved.** Mark it and come back to it. Nobody is checking, which is exactly the problem.
- Chapter 8 was never filmed and is worth more than most of the chapters that were. Read it properly.

## Not scheduled yet, and why

picoCTF and the other practice platforms are listed in the plan from Stage 2 at 3–5 h/week, and there are no such hours in February — the week is already full at fifteen. They start when the PortSwigger track ends, which is the point at which you will also have something to bring to them. Trying to run both now would mean doing neither.

## Where March picks up

Month 6. Nand to Tetris modules 3 to 6 — memory and sequential logic, the Hack machine language, the CPU, and then the assembler. It is the month you finish building a computer and then write the program that programs it, which is also the month the phrase "machine code" stops being an abstraction.

PortSwigger continues at 5 h/week: authentication finished, then access control. [eJPT](../README.md#certifications) sits at months 6 to 7, so March is when the exam gets a date rather than an intention.
