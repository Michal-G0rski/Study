# February 2028 — the second belt, and firmware that fights back

Month 17 of [the plan](../README.md). **15 h/week: 2.5 h/day, Monday to Saturday, Sunday off.**

Scope only — what to read, what to build, what has to exist by Sunday. Setup and tooling are deliberately not here. Standing rules are in [plans/README.md](README.md).

Four clean weeks, sixty hours, no holidays.

Three tracks, the same shape as January. **pwn.college** finishes **Program Security** — the Program Exploitation module and the belt — and opens **System Security**, the next belt up. **[Stage 5](../README.md#stage-5--embedded-and-hardware)** moves from pulling firmware off a board to taking it apart: analysis, emulation, and the first dynamic testing. And **[Microcorruption](https://microcorruption.com/)** runs as the drill — an embedded exploitation CTF, the counterpart to the crackmes habit but on a very different machine.

## Sources in use this month

- [pwn.college](https://pwn.college/dojos) — **Program Exploitation** (finishing Program Security), then **System Security**: introduction, sandboxing, race conditions.
- [OWASP FSTM](https://github.com/scriptingxss/owasp-fstm) stages 5–7, with the tools it names — Firmwalker, FACT, and **EMBA**. Plus [binwalk](https://github.com/ReFirmLabs/binwalk) as the everyday first pass.
- [Microcorruption](https://microcorruption.com/) — the MSP430 embedded exploitation CTF.

> Microcorruption is named here by topic, not by level. Its challenge list sits behind a login and could not be verified, so this file will not invent level names — the same discipline applied to Kurose's later chapters last winter and to RE101 in December. What it is is fixed: a browser-based CTF where you exploit a lock's firmware on the MSP430, a 16-bit microcontroller with a different architecture from anything else in this plan. Work it in its own order. It is the bridge between the x86/ARM exploitation you can do and the small, strange processors embedded work actually lands on.

> System Security is a harder belt than Program Security and its later modules — kernel, microarchitecture — are among the deepest material on the platform. February opens it; it does not finish it, and it was never meant to. Getting the introduction, sandboxing and race-conditions modules done this month is the goal, and race conditions in particular pay back the signal-handling work from CS:APP chapter 8 last August.

> EMBA is a large automated firmware analyser and it will hand you a hundred findings. The skill is not running it; it is reading its output and telling a real finding from noise. Treat its report the way you would treat a subagent's — useful, and not to be trusted at face value.

## Rules for a week that goes wrong

The [standing rules](README.md) apply. Three things specific to this month:

- **The Program Security belt comes before anything else this month.** It is the finish line of the four months since June, and System Security cannot start until it is earned. If week 1 is tight, Microcorruption waits.
- **System Security is not going to finish, and that is fine.** Its kernel and microarchitecture modules are post-plan work. Do not rush the early modules to reach the late ones; the early ones are the ones that transfer to the firmware work sitting right next to them.
- **EMBA's output is a lead, not a finding.** Every automated result gets confirmed by hand before it goes in a writeup. A firmware report full of unverified tool output is worth less than one confirmed bug.
