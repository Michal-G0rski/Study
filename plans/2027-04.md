# April 2027 — CS:APP opens, and the first certificate

Month 7 of [the plan](../README.md). **15 h/week: 2.5 h/day, Monday to Saturday, Sunday off.**

Scope only — what to read, what to build, what has to exist by Sunday. Setup and tooling are deliberately not here. Standing rules are in [plans/README.md](README.md).

Four weeks, fifty-seven and a half hours. 1 May is a public holiday and falls on the Saturday that closes week 4 — the shortest week of the month is the exam week, which is the right way round.

Two things start here. [CS:APP](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~213/) opens, and it is the single largest piece of this plan: it runs from now until August and everything in Stages 3 and 4 assumes it. And the [eJPT](../README.md#certifications) exam happens at the end of the month, which closes the certificate half of the employability track.

## Sources in use this month

- [Computer Systems: A Programmer's Perspective](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~213/), with the [CMU 15-213 schedule](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~213/schedule.html) as the pacing guide and the [self-study labs](http://csapp.cs.cmu.edu/3e/labs.html) as the work. Chapters 1 and 2 this month, and the Data Lab.
- [Web Security Academy](https://portswigger.net/web-security) — file upload vulnerabilities, then SSRF.
- [eJPT](https://ine.com/security/certifications/ejpt-certification/) — the exam, in week 4.

> On the labs: CMU publishes self-study handouts of every lab without solutions, and CS:APP's own page notes that the public binary bomb has its grading-server callback disabled, so you can detonate it as often as you like. The labs are the course. The book without them is a very good book about systems; the book with them is the reason Stage 3 works.

> On chapter 4: it is not scheduled, here or later. CMU's own current schedule does not lecture it either — the Y86-64 processor it builds is ground you covered in Nand to Tetris project 5 three weeks ago, with a machine you designed yourself. Chapter 5 is deferred for a different reason, in [plans/README.md](README.md#what-eighteen-months-actually-holds).

## Week 1 — 5 to 10 April

Split: 10 h CS:APP, 5 h PortSwigger.

Day | Work through
:-- | :--
Mon 5 | CS:APP ch. 1: the tour. What happens to `hello.c` between the file and the pixels — preprocessor, compiler, assembler, linker, loader, and the hardware underneath. Read it as the table of contents it is.
Tue 6 | CS:APP 2.1: information storage — hex, words, byte ordering, and pointers as the addresses they are. The endianness section is the one you will reread; the DNS resolver in January already taught you why.
Wed 7 | CS:APP 2.2: integer representations — unsigned, two's complement, and the conversions between them that the compiler performs without telling you. Then PortSwigger: file upload vulnerabilities.
Thu 8 | CS:APP 2.3, first half: integer arithmetic — addition and its overflow, negation, and the fact that two's complement addition is the same circuit as unsigned addition.
Fri 9 | CS:APP 2.3, second half: multiplication, division by shifting, and the practice problems. Do them on paper. PortSwigger: file upload — the labs where the filter checks the wrong thing.
Sat 10 | The practice problems from 2.1 to 2.3 you skipped, done properly. PortSwigger: **file upload closed**, web shells included.

**Done by Sunday**

- [ ] CS:APP chapter 1, and 2.1 to 2.3 with the practice problems
- [ ] PortSwigger: file upload vulnerabilities closed
- [ ] A written answer: in November you wrote a program that read a binary format. Find one place in it where you relied on an integer conversion you had not thought about, and say what CS:APP 2.2 tells you it actually did
- [ ] Six commits

## Rules for a week that goes wrong

The [standing rules](README.md) apply. Three things specific to this month:

- **The Data Lab does not move.** It is the prerequisite for reading disassembly in May, and reading disassembly is the prerequisite for everything in Stage 3. If the reading slips, the reading slips.
- **The exam week is the exam week.** Do not schedule CS:APP into it as a hedge. Two half-done things is the failure mode here.
- **If the exam is failed, it is failed and rebooked, and the plan does not change.** The [checkpoint](../README.md#employability-checkpoint--months-910) in June leans on the certificate, but it stands on the PortSwigger track and the writeups too. A retake in May costs one week, not a month.

## Where May picks up

Month 8. CS:APP chapter 3 — the machine-level representation of programs — which is four lectures at CMU and is the chapter this entire plan has been walking toward. Then the **Bomb Lab**: a binary you did not write, that you defuse by reading its disassembly.

That is the first genuine reverse engineering in this plan, and the writeup of it is the [portfolio milestone](../README.md#portfolio-and-visibility) the main plan names for Stage 2.

PortSwigger also closes in May — XXE, then the client-side pair, XSS and CSRF — and the five hours it has held every week since February go to the [checkpoint](../README.md#employability-checkpoint--months-910) from June onward.
