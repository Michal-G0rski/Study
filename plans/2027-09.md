# September 2027 — x86-64, at the level a reverser needs it

Month 12 of [the plan](../README.md). **15 h/week: 2.5 h/day, Monday to Saturday, Sunday off.**

Scope only — what to read, what to build, what has to exist by Sunday. Setup and tooling are deliberately not here. Standing rules are in [plans/README.md](README.md).

Four clean weeks, sixty hours, no holidays.

[Arch1001](https://ost2.fyi/Arch1001) carries the month and finishes in it. CS:APP chapter 3 taught you to read the assembly a compiler emits from C you can imagine. This course goes at the same machine from the other end — instruction by instruction, with the Intel manual open — which is the situation you are in every time you open something you did not build.

## Sources in use this month

- [OpenSecurityTraining2 — Architecture 1001: x86-64 Assembly](https://ost2.fyi/Arch1001) — chapters *Calling Functions* through *Conclusion*, in the course's own order.
- The [Intel Software Developer's Manual](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/developer/articles/technical/intel-sdk-manuals.html), volume 2, as a lookup — never as reading. The course has a chapter called *Learning to Fish: Read The F\*n Intel Manual!* and it means it.
- [picoCTF](https://cylabacademy.org) — reverse engineering, the last month before it hands over to [crackmes](https://crackmes.one/).

> The course branches. After *CISC Delight: REPeatable Instructions* there is a chapter called *Choose Your Own Adventure!*, and it splits into Windows debugging and a twenty-four-part Linux walk through the same examples. **Take the Linux branch and do not do both** — the Linux chapter is a replay of the Windows examples, not new material. You work on Linux; take the branch you will keep using.

> The course ends with its own binary bomb lab. You defused one in May. That is not a scheduling error and you do not skip it: you do it again, faster, in a debugger, and the speed difference between May and September is the clearest measurement of progress this plan contains. The optional buffer-overflow lab at the end is the Attack Lab in miniature — do it if the week has room, skip it without guilt if it does not.

> The course states one prerequisite: *"You must be comfortable with C programming."* Eleven months in, you are. **The course publishes no hour count** — no OST2 course does — so the four-week budget here is an estimate from the chapter list, not a figure from the site. If it runs long, see the rules below.

Split: 10 h Arch1001, 5 h picoCTF.

Day | Work through
:-- | :--
Mon 6 | Arch1001: *Calling Functions*. The `call` and `ret` pair at the instruction level, and what the stack looks like across the boundary.

## Rules for a week that goes wrong

The [standing rules](README.md) apply. Three things specific to this month:

- **The course publishes no hour count, so the budget above is an estimate.** If Arch1001 is not finished by 2 October, it takes the first two weeks of October and Ghidra moves back. That is an acceptable slip. Opening [pwn.college](https://pwn.college/) before you can read assembly fluently is not.
- **Do not do both branches.** The Windows chapter and the Linux chapter are the same examples. Doing both costs a week and teaches nothing twice.
- **The pattern library and the reference are deliverables, not notes.** They are the two files from this month you will still be using at the end of the plan.

## Not scheduled, and why

**[Arch2001 — x86-64 OS Internals](https://ost2.fyi/Arch2001)** is in the main plan and is not scheduled anywhere in these eighteen months. Its published chapters are privilege rings and segmentation, interrupts, system calls, paging, MSRs, port I/O — and its sub-lessons carry question counts in the hundreds, so it is a far heavier course than Arch1001 despite having fewer chapters. The eighteen months do not contain both it and a real Stage 4.

Its subject is met twice over here already: as theory in CS:APP chapter 9, and applied in the kernel modules of pwn.college from February. It is the first thing to pick up after month 18 if kernel work turns out to be the direction — and its stated prerequisite, *"you must have taken OST2 Architecture 1001, or have equivalent knowledge of assembly"*, is satisfied by the end of this month either way. The full accounting is in [plans/README.md](README.md#what-eighteen-months-actually-holds).
