# August 2027 — CS:APP closes, Stage 3 opens

Month 11 of [the plan](../README.md). **15 h/week: 2.5 h/day, Monday to Saturday, Sunday off.**

Scope only — what to read, what to build, what has to exist by Sunday. Setup and tooling are deliberately not here. Standing rules are in [plans/README.md](README.md).

Five weeks, seventy-five hours, no holidays. The longest working month in the whole plan, and it is the month the biggest book in it finishes.

From here the shape changes. Until now there has almost always been one large course carrying the week. From [Stage 3](../README.md#stage-3--low-level-and-assembly) onward there are several tracks at once and at least a third of the time is hands-on — the main plan's own instruction from Stage 3 on.

Applications no longer have a scheduled block. The [checkpoint](../README.md#employability-checkpoint--months-910) block closed in July; from now on applying is something you do when a posting appears, and the log keeps being updated.

## Sources in use this month

- [Computer Systems: A Programmer's Perspective](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~213/) — chapters 8, 10 and 12. The book finishes this month.
- [OpenSecurityTraining2 — Architecture 1001: x86-64 Assembly](https://ost2.fyi/Arch1001) — opens in week 5. Its one stated prerequisite is being comfortable with C, which eleven months have settled.
- [picoCTF](https://cylabacademy.org) — the reverse-engineering category, which is the bridge to crackmes in October.

> On chapter 11, network programming: it is not scheduled. You spent December and January on Kurose and Beej, and you wrote a TCP server, a DNS resolver that builds its own packets and a traceroute that reads ICMP replies. Chapter 11 teaches sockets with a helper library; you already did it the hard way. Nothing is lost here.

> On chapter 5, optimising program performance: also not scheduled, and this one is a genuine trade rather than a duplicate. It is a compiler-and-pipeline chapter aimed at making code fast, and this path is aimed at making code do something it was not supposed to. See [plans/README.md](README.md#what-eighteen-months-actually-holds).

## Rules for a week that goes wrong

The [standing rules](README.md) apply. Three things specific to this month:

- **Chapter 8 does not get skimmed.** Processes and signals are the substrate of the sandboxing and race-condition work in Stage 4, and of every `fork`-and-exec bug you will ever find in firmware.
- **Five weeks is not permission to go slower.** August is long because the calendar made it long, and September is short.
- **The CS:APP review is a deliverable.** It is the only place the five months get looked at as a whole, and you will read it again in March 2028.

## Where September picks up

Month 12. Arch1001 at full weight, and it finishes. x86-64 assembly the way someone who reverses binaries needs it, rather than the way someone who writes C needs it — which is the difference between chapter 3 and this course.

The hands-on hours stay where they are, and in October they become [crackmes](https://crackmes.one/), once Ghidra is in your hands.
