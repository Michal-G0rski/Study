# January 2028 — return-oriented programming, for real this time

Month 16 of [the plan](../README.md). **15 h/week: 2.5 h/day, Monday to Saturday, Sunday off.**

Scope only — what to read, what to build, what has to exist by Sunday. Setup and tooling are deliberately not here. Standing rules are in [plans/README.md](README.md).

Five weeks, seventy-two and a half hours. One public holiday — 6 January opens week 1 short — and the rest full. It is the heaviest exploitation month in the plan, and everything since June was preparing for it.

Three tracks. **pwn.college** reaches **Return Oriented Programming** and **Dynamic Allocator Misuse** — the two modules the Attack Lab and the Malloc Lab were the rehearsal for. **[ROP Emporium](https://ropemporium.com/)** runs alongside as the drill, across more than one architecture. And **[Stage 5](../README.md#stage-5--embedded-and-hardware)** turns the ESP32 from a datasheet into a board: the buses, and the first firmware you pull off hardware yourself.

And once this month, a decision rather than a lesson: **OSCP**.

## Sources in use this month

- [pwn.college](https://pwn.college/dojos) — Program Security's **Return Oriented Programming** and **Dynamic Allocator Misuse** modules.
- [ROP Emporium](https://ropemporium.com/) — the ladder in order: ret2win, split, callme, write4, badchars, fluff, pivot, ret2csu. Done in x86-64 first, then the foundations repeated on another architecture.
- [The Hardware Hacking Handbook](https://nostarch.com/hardwarehacking) ch. 2–3 and [Practical IoT Hacking](https://nostarch.com/practical-iot-hacking) ch. 7–9 — the peripheral buses and firmware hacking. Plus [OWASP FSTM](https://github.com/scriptingxss/owasp-fstm) stages 1–4, for the extraction method.

> ROP Emporium ships in four architectures — x86, x86-64, ARMv5 and MIPS — and this month uses that on purpose. Do the whole ladder in x86-64, then repeat the first three challenges on ARMv5 and MIPS, because Stage 5 targets are ARM and MIPS, not x86. One caveat that will otherwise waste an afternoon: **ret2csu has no 32-bit x86 build** — only 64-bit, ARMv5 and MIPS — so a plan to do the full eight-rung ladder in 32-bit x86 hits a wall at the last rung. Do the ladder in x86-64, where all eight exist.

> pwn.college's ROP module and ROP Emporium are not redundant. The dojo teaches the technique against its own challenge set with a belt on the line; ROP Emporium is a clean, escalating drill with a writeup expected at each rung. Doing both is how the technique stops being something you follow in a walkthrough and becomes something you reach for.

> Dynamic Allocator Misuse assumes you built an allocator. You did, in July — the Malloc Lab. Open your own allocator's source next to this module. The metadata you are about to corrupt is the metadata you wrote.

## The OSCP decision

The main plan schedules [OSCP](../README.md#certifications) for months 14–18. This plan does not schedule it, and week 1 is where you decide what to do about that — deliberately, in writing, not by drift.

The reason it is a decision and not a lesson: PEN-200, the course behind the OSCP, is a full-load, roughly three-month commitment with a punishing 24-hour exam at the end. It does not fit fifteen hours a week alongside a live Stage 4 and Stage 5. Something has to give, and pretending otherwise is how people half-do three things instead of finishing one.

So the decision has three honest answers, and June's [re-measured market](../README.md#market-snapshot-september-2026) numbers pick between them:

- **Go now** — if you are not yet employed and the postings you want name OSCP as a filter. Then it becomes the plan: Stages 4 and 5 pause, PEN-200 runs full-time, and these month files resume after the exam.
- **Defer** — if you are employed, or close, and OSCP would be a raise rather than a door. Then it is the first thing after month 18, on a schedule that is not competing with anything.
- **Drop** — if the roles you are actually landing are reverse-engineering, embedded or research roles, where a portfolio of the work you have been publishing all year outweighs a pentest cert you would have to force into the calendar.

Write the answer down, with the reason, and the one change in circumstances that would flip it. That written answer is the deliverable — not the certificate.
