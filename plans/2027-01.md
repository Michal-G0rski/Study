## Rules for a week that goes wrong

- The three attacks (Kerberoasting, AS-REP, DCSync) are the month. Lectures can slip; the labs cannot.
- Stage 1 review is not a formality and does not move. Four months is exactly long enough to forget what you did in the first one.
- **Week 5 stays a consolidation week.** If it's tempting to start the Sentinel/KQL work early, don't — February opens it properly, and running ahead here just means re-reading the same modules twice.
- **The market spot-check decides whether Stage 2 opens on momentum or on numbers.** February to April — detection logic, Wazuh, ATT&CK, incident triage, phishing — transfers to any blue-team seat whatever the SIEM, so it opens regardless. The Microsoft-specific bet is May–June (SC-200, Defender, Entra), and the money behind it is April's exam booking. If the SOC L1 count is in September's range (~14), change nothing. If it has fallen to half or less, re-read the [checkpoint](../README.md#employability-checkpoint--month-11) and the [QA fallback](../README.md#if-the-checkpoint-itself-doesnt-land), and write down — before April — whether the SC-200 block stays as planned. If the SOC postings mostly name Splunk rather than Sentinel, that is the [Splunk note](README.md#what-was-deferred-and-why) pulled forward, not a new program.
