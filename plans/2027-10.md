# October 2027 — Ghidra, and Stage 4 opens

Month 13 of [the plan](../README.md). **15 h/week: 2.5 h/day, Monday to Saturday, Sunday off.**

Scope only — what to read, what to build, what has to exist by Sunday. Setup and tooling are deliberately not here. Standing rules are in [plans/README.md](README.md).

Four clean weeks, sixty hours, no holidays.

Two things open and neither one closes this month. **Ghidra**, because reading disassembly by hand stops scaling somewhere around three hundred instructions and you are already past that. And **[pwn.college](https://pwn.college/)**, which is [Stage 4](../README.md#stage-4--offense) and which runs from here to the end of the plan — the backbone of the second half in the way CS:APP was the backbone of the first.

## Sources in use this month

- [OpenSecurityTraining2 — Debuggers 1102: Introductory Ghidra](https://ost2.fyi/Dbg1102) — a mini-course, finished in three weeks. Its stated prerequisites are knowledge of a debugger and of the assembly you want to read; September gave you both.
- [pwn.college](https://pwn.college/dojos) — the Getting Started dojos: **Start Here**, **Linux Luminarium**, **Computing 101**, **Playing With Programs**.
- [crackmes.one](https://crackmes.one/) — opens here at two and a half hours a week, and takes over from picoCTF as the reverse-engineering practice for the rest of the plan.

> pwn.college is explicit that *"the material is designed to be tackled in order"* and that on the Core Material you *"must earn belts sequentially"*. You do not skip ahead to the exploitation dojos because the early ones look easy. They are easy — for you specifically, after a year of C and a year of Linux — and that is the point: you clear them fast and arrive at the belts having actually used the platform.

> Most of **Linux Luminarium** and a good half of **Computing 101** are revision. *The Stack*, *Endian Escapades*, *Assembly Assortment*, *Debugging Refresher* are things you have done twice. Move through them at the speed they deserve and spend the time you save on the modules that are not revision — *Perceiving Permissions*, *Untangling Users*, *Silly Shenanigans*.

> The Ghidra course opens with install and test-executable chapters. They are short, and they are not the reason the course is here. The reason is the two UI chapters — the Code Browser and the Debugger — which are thirty-two sub-lessons between them and are the difference between having Ghidra open and being able to use it.

## Rules for a week that goes wrong

The [standing rules](README.md) apply. Three things specific to this month:

- **The script is not optional.** Anyone can post a Ghidra screenshot. A script is evidence that you understood the program well enough to describe the pattern, and it is the only Stage 3 artifact that a reverse-engineering employer cannot get from a tutorial.
- **Do not let pwn.college slip.** It has five more months of work in it and every one of them assumes the one before. The Getting Started dojos are the cheapest they will ever be; spend the hours now.
- **If Arch1001 ran over into October**, Ghidra moves back and pwn.college does not. The platform's sequence is the fixed thing here.
