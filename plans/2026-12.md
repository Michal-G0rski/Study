# December 2026 — Stage 1 continues, Stage 2 opens

Month 3 of [the plan](../README.md). **Normal weeks are 15 h: 2.5 h/day, Monday to Saturday, Sunday off.**

Scope only — what to read, what to write, what has to exist by Sunday. Setup and tooling are deliberately not here. Standing rules are in [plans/README.md](README.md).

**Two short weeks.** 24, 25 and 26 December are public holidays, and so is 1 January. Week 3 is three days and week 4 is four. That makes 47.5 hours in the month instead of 60, and it is planned that way — do not try to make it up in January.

This is also the month the [portfolio track](../README.md#portfolio-and-visibility) starts.

## Sources in use this month

- [Computer Networking: a Top-Down Approach, online lectures](https://gaia.cs.umass.edu/kurose_ross/online_lectures.htm) — Kurose and Ross, chapters 1 to 3. The video pages track the 9th edition.
- [Beej on network programming](https://beej.us/guide/bgnet/html/) — chapters 1 to 7, read alongside the lectures rather than after them.

Modern C is finished. It does not disappear: every program this month is written in it.

Day | Work through
:-- | :--
Mon 7 | Kurose 1.1 and 1.2: what the Internet is, and the network edge — access networks and physical media.
Tue 8 | Kurose 1.3: the network core — packet switching against circuit switching, and why the argument ended the way it did.
Wed 9 | Kurose 1.4: delay, loss and throughput. The four sources of delay are the whole of performance work later on.
Thu 10 | Kurose 1.5 and 1.6: protocol layers and service models, then networks under attack.
Fri 11 | Kurose 1.7 and the chapter summary. Beej ch. 1–3: what a socket is, and addresses as data — read closely, not skimmed.
Sat 12 | Take a capture of your own traffic and name every layer from 1.5 in it, for one HTTP request, byte by byte.

## Rules for a week that goes wrong

The [standing rules](README.md) apply. Three things specific to this month:

- The short weeks are short on purpose. Nothing moves out of week 3 or week 4 into January.
- Section 3.4 is the densest material in the book and the one that pays off most in Stage 4. If it wants three days instead of two, give it three and let Beej slip.
- The blog post is not the thing that gets dropped when the week is tight. In this niche it counts for more than the week of lectures around it — see [Market snapshot](../README.md#market-snapshot-september-2026).

## Where January picks up

[January](2027-01.md) finishes the transport layer and takes the network layer and the link layer: TCP and congestion control, IP and routers, routing algorithms and BGP, then Ethernet and switches. Stage 1 ends on 31 January.
