# December 2027 — Stage 5 opens, and the year ends on a chip

Month 15 of [the plan](../README.md). **15 h/week: 2.5 h/day, Monday to Saturday, Sunday off.**

Scope only — what to read, what to build, what has to exist by Sunday. Setup and tooling are deliberately not here. Standing rules are in [plans/README.md](README.md).

Four weeks, fifty-two and a half hours. Three public holidays land on study days — 24 and 25 December sit inside week 3, and 1 January closes week 4 — so those two weeks are short and the first two are full. It is the lightest month in the plan, and it is deliberately the one where the new hardware arrives, because opening a new stage in a short month means opening it slowly.

Two things carry over and one thing starts. **pwn.college Program Security** continues, with its reversing module pointed at a malware primer. And **[Stage 5](../README.md#stage-5--embedded-and-hardware) opens** — the ESP32, the first time in fifteen months that software stops attacking software and starts attacking a chip.

## Sources in use this month

- [pwn.college](https://pwn.college/dojos) — **Program Security**, continuing, including its *Reverse Engineering* module.
- [Malware Unicorn RE101](https://malwareunicorn.org/workshops/re101.html) — the reverse-engineering-for-malware workshop, run alongside pwn.college's reversing so the two reinforce each other.
- [ESP-IDF Programming Guide](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/stable/esp32/index.html) — specifically the **Security Guides**, and a first firmware image to read. Not the install guide; the security architecture.

> On the RE101 module list: this file names it by topic, not by section, because its exact syllabus was not verified against the live workshop the way the pwn.college and ESP-IDF material was. Work through it in its own published order. What it is *for* here is fixed even if its headings are not — static and dynamic analysis of a hostile binary, the standard tooling, and the anti-analysis tricks that make malware harder to read than a crackme. It is the reversing you have been building, aimed at the thing reversing is actually paid to do.

> On the ESP32: the scope here is the chip's security architecture, not its toolchain. Read the ESP-IDF **Security Guides** — secure boot, flash encryption, and the threat model they assume — and get one firmware image into a hex editor. Buying the board and installing the SDK belong to the separate setup project, not to this file. What belongs here is understanding what secure boot actually protects and where it does not.

> Program Security is a sequential belt and it does not pause for the holidays. If a short week forces a cut, the RE101 hours give way before the dojo does — the same rule as every month since October.

## Rules for a week that goes wrong

The [standing rules](README.md) apply. Three things specific to this month:

- **The month is light on purpose; do not fill it.** Do not run ahead into January's ESP32 work. A new stage opened in a slow month is meant to be opened slowly, and running ahead here just means arriving at February's hard exploitation tired.
- **RE101's dynamic analysis runs real malware. Treat the isolation as load-bearing.** The workshop's safety instructions are the scope, not a preamble to it. If you cannot follow them, do the static half only and say so in the writeup.
- **The ESP32 scope is security architecture, not setup.** If you find yourself installing a toolchain, you have crossed into the other project. Read what secure boot protects; leave the flashing to the setup you do on Monday morning.
