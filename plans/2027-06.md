# June 2027 — SOC basics, and the checkpoint opens

Month 9 of [the plan](../README.md). **15 h/week: 2.5 h/day, Monday to Saturday, Sunday off.**

Scope only — what to read, what to write, what has to exist by Sunday. Setup and tooling are deliberately not here. Standing rules are in [plans/README.md](README.md).

Four weeks plus a day, sixty-five hours. 6 June falls on a Sunday; no public holidays land on study days.

Two things, and the second is the one the whole plan was built to reach. First, **SOC basics**: the Microsoft Sentinel learning path, MITRE ATT&CK, and the alert-triage vocabulary — the unit that used to be a conditional fallback and is now scheduled, because SOC / Detection Engineer (L1) is the largest genuinely junior slice of the security market and the plan's minimum list names it as core. Second, the [**employability checkpoint**](../README.md#employability-checkpoint--month-9) opens: CV, public profile, the market re-measured, and the first applications actually sent.

After this month there is no more fixed schedule. The deep low-level curriculum — architecture, reverse engineering, exploitation, embedded work — is the [backlog](../README.md#after-the-checkpoint), picked up in whatever order the job you land demands.

## Sources in use this month

- [Microsoft Sentinel learning path](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/training/paths/sc-200-configure-azure-sentinel-environment/) — configuring a workspace, then creating detections. Free, hands-on-ish, and the SIEM vocabulary a triage seat runs on.
- [MITRE ATT&CK](https://attack.mitre.org/) — the framework, as vocabulary rather than as reading. Know the tactics cold; recognize the techniques by family.
- The [checkpoint](../README.md#employability-checkpoint--month-9) itself, and the [market snapshot](../README.md#market-snapshot-september-2026) from September 2026, which exists to be re-measured.

> The SOC unit is deliberately short. It is not a third program of study — it is the vocabulary and one SIEM's workflow, enough to sit in a triage interview and speak. The networking foundation (Kurose, Beej) and the Linux and toolchain grounding (the Missing Semester) are already in place from months 1–4; that is most of what a L1 seat assumes.

## Rules for a week that goes wrong

The [standing rules](README.md) apply. Three things specific to this month:

- **Applications do not slip.** They are the one item here with an outside clock on them, and the whole argument for the checkpoint is arithmetic: getting hired at month 9 turns the backlog from unpaid study into paid study. A week of applications postponed is a week of that postponed.
- **Do not wait until you feel ready.** You will not. The main plan anticipated this and wrote the instruction down on purpose.
- **The SOC unit is vocabulary, not a career.** If it runs long, cut it short and send the applications. Nobody was ever hired for a Sentinel lab exercise; the interviews are won by the C, the protocol programs and the writeups.

## What comes after month 9

Nothing scheduled — and that is the design. The [backlog](../README.md#after-the-checkpoint) holds the deep curriculum: Nand to Tetris, CS:APP with the four labs, OSTEP, assembly and reverse engineering, pwn.college and the exploitation stack, the ESP32 and all the firmware work. The order you pick it up in is decided by the job you land, not by a calendar — the reasoning is in the [main plan](../README.md#the-order-to-pick-them-up-in), and the default if nothing has landed yet is CS:APP.

It is not an unfinished plan. It is a nine-month plan that ends in applications, and a backlog that begins with a job.
