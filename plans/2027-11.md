# November 2027 — ARM, and the first belt

Month 14 of [the plan](../README.md). **15 h/week: 2.5 h/day, Monday to Saturday, Sunday off.**

Scope only — what to read, what to build, what has to exist by Sunday. Setup and tooling are deliberately not here. Standing rules are in [plans/README.md](README.md).

Five weeks, seventy hours. Two public holidays land on study days — 1 November opens week 1 and 11 November sits inside week 2 — so those two weeks run at twelve and a half hours and the other three are full.

Two tracks. **ARM**, because everything in [Stage 5](../README.md#stage-5--embedded-and-hardware) runs on it and nothing you learned in September carries over directly. And **pwn.college**, which moves off the Getting Started dojos and onto the [Core Material](https://pwn.college/dojos) — the first belt this month, and Program Security opened behind it.

## Sources in use this month

- [Azeria Labs — Writing ARM Assembly](https://azeria-labs.com/writing-arm-assembly-part-1/) — the seven-part assembly series and the cheatsheet, then the first of the exploitation tutorials.
- [pwn.college](https://pwn.college/dojos) — **Intro to Cybersecurity** (the first belt), then **Program Security** opened in week 5.
- [crackmes.one](https://crackmes.one/) — continuing, and the running count reaches ten this month.

> ARM is not x86 with different names. It is fixed-length where x86 is variable-length, load/store where x86 lets arithmetic touch memory directly, and it has a link register where x86 puts the return address on the stack. September's instinct — decode the bytes, find the ModRM — does not apply. Learn ARM as its own thing and the transfer of skill is in the method, not the mnemonics.

> pwn.college's Intro to Cybersecurity is a breadth dojo: web, crypto, access control, reversing, a first taste of binary exploitation, all in one belt. Most of it you have met — PortSwigger did the web, the Attack Lab did the exploitation. Clear the modules you know quickly and slow down on *Cryptography* and *Intercepting Communication*, which are the two you have not done before.

> The two exploitation tutorials at the end of the month — shellcode and a first ARM stack overflow — are a preview, not the syllabus. The full ARM exploitation series is [Stage 5](../README.md#stage-5--embedded-and-hardware) work and it waits for the new year. This month is about being able to *read* ARM; breaking it comes later.

## Rules for a week that goes wrong

The [standing rules](README.md) apply. Three things specific to this month:

- **pwn.college's order is not negotiable.** You earn belts sequentially and the modules assume each other. If a week is short, ARM gives way before the dojo does — you can read ARM slowly later, but a half-finished belt blocks everything after it.
- **The ARM reference is a deliverable.** It is the file that makes Stage 5 possible without relearning the architecture in January, and it is worth the hour it costs to write.
- **Ten crackmes is a floor, not a target.** The number is there so the habit is visible on the blog. If you are solving them faster than one a week, solve more; the notes are what matter, not the count.
