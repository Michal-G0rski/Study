# May 2027 — build it, attack it, detect it

Month 8 of [the plan](../README.md). **15 h/week: 2.5 h/day, Monday to Saturday, Sunday off.**

Scope only — what to read, what to build, what has to exist by Sunday. Setup and tooling are deliberately not here. Standing rules are in [plans/README.md](README.md).

Five weeks, seventy hours. Two public holidays land on study days — 3 May opens week 1 and 27 May sits inside week 4 — so those two weeks run at twelve and a half hours and the other three are full.

Three hats this month, in order. Weeks 1 and 2: **build** a real web application — authentication, sessions, a data layer, deployed and working, with security-relevant events logged from the first day. Week 3: **freeze and attack** it — SQL injection, XSS, brute force, path traversal — while capturing what each attack looks like in its logs. Week 4: **fix and detect** — close the bugs, then write the detections that would have caught the attacks, and publish the whole story. The built-attacked-detected writeup is the portfolio piece a SOC hiring manager reads twice: it is one person doing the attack, the defense, and the detection rule that connects them.

## Sources in use this month

- Your own application. Small, real, and yours — a login, a session, a place data goes in and comes out, and a log for every security-relevant event. Any stack; the point is the shapes real bugs live in, not the framework.
- The networking, HTTP and log vocabulary from months 1–7, turned back on your own work.

> Write the application the way you would if you were not about to attack it — and log like someone who will have to detect it. A planted bug teaches nothing; an unplanted one that survives to week 3 is the whole deliverable.

## Rules for a week that goes wrong

The [standing rules](README.md) apply. Three things specific to this month:

- **The app has to freeze on schedule.** Week 3 cannot attack what week 3 is still building. If the build runs long, cut features, never the freeze.
- **Do not plant bugs.** The writeup is honest or it is worthless — and finding none is a real result to write up too.
- **The detections are the deliverable, not the bugs.** Anyone can break a web app; the junior a SOC wants is the one who can write the rule that catches it.
