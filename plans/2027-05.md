# May 2027 — build it, then break it

Month 8 of [the plan](../README.md). **15 h/week: 2.5 h/day, Monday to Saturday, Sunday off.**

Scope only — what to read, what to build, what has to exist by Sunday. Setup and tooling are deliberately not here. Standing rules are in [plans/README.md](README.md).

Five weeks, seventy hours. Two public holidays land on study days — 3 May opens week 1 and 27 May sits inside week 4 — so those two weeks run at twelve and a half hours and the other three are full.

This is the month both hats go on. Weeks 1 and 2: build a real web application — authentication, sessions, a data layer, deployed and working. Week 3: freeze it and break it with the entire PortSwigger toolkit. Week 4: fix what you found, confirm the fixes the way an attacker would, and publish the writeup. The build-and-break writeup is the portfolio piece an Application Security Engineer employer reads to the end, and it is the [checkpoint](../README.md#employability-checkpoint--month-9) month's second-strongest card after the certificate.

## Sources in use this month

- Your own application. Small, real, and yours — a login, a session, a place data goes in and comes out. Any stack; the point is the shapes real bugs live in, not the framework.
- The [Web Security Academy](https://portswigger.net/web-security) skills from February and March, turned back on your own work.

> Write the application the way you would if you were not about to attack it. The honest bugs are the interesting ones — a planted bug teaches nothing, and an unplanted one that survives to week 3 is the whole deliverable.

Day | Work through

Sat 8 | Consolidate the week: what works, what is stubbed, what week 2 has to finish.

**Done by Sunday**

## Week 3 — 17 to 22 May

Split: all on the break.

Day | Work through
:-- | :--
Mon 17 | Attack your own app with the PortSwigger toolkit — authentication first. Find the bugs you left without meaning to.
Tue 18 | Access control and IDOR: every object in the app, asked for by the wrong user.
Wed 19 | Injection surfaces: the data layer you built on Monday of week 2. Then information disclosure — errors, debug output, anything the app says that it should not.
Thu 20 | Business logic: the flows, in order, asking what happens if the user does the steps out of order or not at all.
Fri 21 | Keep going. The interesting bugs are the ones you did not plant. For each, write what it is, how you found it, and what the fix is.
Sat 22 | The bug list, consolidated and committed. Rank them: what an attacker would use first.

**Done by Sunday**

- [ ] Your own app attacked with the full February–March toolkit
- [ ] The bug list, written and committed — each bug with its finding story and its fix
- [ ] Six commits

## Rules for a week that goes wrong

The [standing rules](README.md) apply. Three things specific to this month:

- **The app has to freeze on schedule.** Week 3 cannot break what week 3 is still building. If the build runs long, cut features, never the freeze.
- **Do not plant bugs.** The writeup is honest or it is worthless — an unplanted bug found by your own toolkit is the entire point, and finding none is a real result to write up too.
- **The writeup is a deliverable, not a bonus.** It is the artifact the [checkpoint](../README.md#employability-checkpoint--month-9) sends Application Security Engineer postings to.
