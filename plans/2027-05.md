# May 2027 — chapter 3, and the first binary you did not write

Month 8 of [the plan](../README.md). **15 h/week: 2.5 h/day, Monday to Saturday, Sunday off.**

Scope only — what to read, what to build, what has to exist by Sunday. Setup and tooling are deliberately not here. Standing rules are in [plans/README.md](README.md).

Five weeks, seventy hours. Two public holidays land on study days — 3 May opens week 1 and 27 May sits inside week 4 — so those two weeks run at twelve and a half hours and the other three are full.

This is the month the plan turns. Everything until now you wrote yourself; the Bomb Lab is a program someone else wrote, that you do not have the source for, that you have to understand anyway. That is the job description for most of Stages 3, 4 and 5.

## Sources in use this month

- [Computer Systems: A Programmer's Perspective](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~213/) — chapter 3 end to end, and the **Bomb Lab** from the [self-study labs](http://csapp.cs.cmu.edu/3e/labs.html).
- [Web Security Academy](https://portswigger.net/web-security) — XXE injection, then cross-site scripting and CSRF. The track closes this month.

> Chapter 3 is four lectures at CMU and it is the densest reading in this plan. It is also the chapter with the highest ratio of payoff to pages: section 3.10 alone contains buffer overflow, stack canaries, ASLR and non-executable stacks, which is the entire threat model that Stage 4 spends four months attacking.

> The Bomb Lab has six phases and each one is a different kind of puzzle — a string, a number, a loop, a recursive function, a lookup table, a linked list. Phase 6 defeats more people than the other five together. Budget for it.

## Rules for a week that goes wrong

The [standing rules](README.md) apply. Three things specific to this month:

- **Do not look up bomb phase solutions.** Every phase of every bomb is on the internet and every one of them is worthless to you. This is the lab that decides whether Stage 3 is possible, and it decides it by what you did when you were stuck.
- **Chapter 3.10 is the load-bearing section of the whole book for this plan.** If something in chapter 3 has to be skimmed, skim 3.11. Never 3.10.
- **The writeup is a deliverable, not a bonus.** It is the first artifact on the blog that is about reading a binary, and it is what the [checkpoint](../README.md#employability-checkpoint--months-910) next month actually sends people to.

## What the PortSwigger track leaves open

The Academy has more topics than four months at five hours a week can close, and it always did. Closed here: SQL injection, authentication, path traversal, command injection, access control, information disclosure, business logic, file upload, SSRF, XXE, XSS, CSRF.

Not closed, deliberately: insecure deserialization, web cache poisoning and deception, GraphQL, NoSQL injection, race conditions, WebSockets, clickjacking, CORS, web LLM attacks, API testing. They are the advanced and specialist half of the Academy and none of them is on the path to [the roles this plan targets](../README.md#target-roles). They stay available for free, and they are worth a week each if an application ever asks for one.

What the track was for is now done: a certificate, a set of topics you can defend in an interview, and something to apply with in June.
