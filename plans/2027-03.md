# March 2027 — Stage 2, the machine gets built

Month 6 of [the plan](../README.md). **15 h/week: 2.5 h/day, Monday to Saturday, Sunday off.**

Scope only — what to read, what to build, what has to exist by Sunday. Setup and tooling are deliberately not here. Standing rules are in [plans/README.md](README.md).

Five weeks, seventy-two and a half hours. Easter Monday falls on 29 March and is the only day out; it lands on the first day of week 5, which is the lightest week of the month for exactly that reason.

This is the month a computer exists at the end of. Not a simulation of one in your head — a CPU you specified gate by gate, running a program you assembled with an assembler you wrote.

## Sources in use this month

- [Nand to Tetris Part I](https://www.coursera.org/learn/build-a-computer) — modules 3, 4, 5 and 6, and projects 3 to 6. Part I finishes this month.
- [Web Security Academy](https://portswigger.net/web-security) — authentication finished, then access control, information disclosure, and business logic vulnerabilities.
- [eJPT](https://ine.com/security/certifications/ejpt-certification/) — no study hours this month. The exam gets booked, which is the whole point of the entry.

> Project 3 is the one that catches people. Everything up to now has been combinational — output is a function of input and nothing else. A flip-flop is the first chip whose output depends on *when* you ask. The course hands you the DFF and asks you to build everything above it; take the day it costs to accept that you are not allowed to look inside it.

## Rules for a week that goes wrong

The [standing rules](README.md) apply. Three things specific to this month:

- **The projects are the month, and they are sequential.** Project 5 cannot be built on a broken project 3. If a chip fails its test script, that is the day's work, however far behind it puts the reading.
- **Do not read other people's HDL.** This is the one course in the plan where the solutions are all over GitHub and looking costs you the entire value of the exercise. A gate you copied teaches nothing; a gate that took you two hours is yours permanently.
- PortSwigger keeps its five hours, as in February, and for the same reason: [eJPT](../README.md#certifications) is in April and the [checkpoint](../README.md#employability-checkpoint--months-910) is in June.

## Not scheduled, and why

- **Nand to Tetris Part II** is not scheduled this month and is not scheduled later in this plan. It is a compiler course — VM translator, Jack language, compiler, OS — and the machine-level payoff of the series is in Part I. The one part of it that matters here, how a stack machine actually works, arrives again in CS:APP chapter 3 and again in every stack frame you smash in Stage 4. It is worth doing after this plan, not inside it.
- **The main plan lists Part II as a prerequisite for [OSTEP](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~remzi/OSTEP/)**, which follows it into the deferred column. See [plans/README.md](README.md#what-eighteen-months-actually-holds) for where the operating-systems material goes instead — it is not dropped, it moves into CS:APP chapters 8, 9, 10 and 12.

## Where April picks up

Month 7. [CS:APP](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~213/) opens and does not close until August — it is the largest single thing in this plan and the one that Stages 3 and 4 both stand on. April takes chapter 1, chapter 2 and the Data Lab, which is bit-level manipulation under a restriction that makes it a puzzle rather than an exercise.

And the [eJPT](../README.md#certifications) exam happens. The last week of April is the exam, not the preparation for it.
