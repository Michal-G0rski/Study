# July 2027 — Stage 2, Attack-Chain Lab: Attack → Detect Twice Over

Month 10 of [the plan](../README.md). **15 h/week: 2.5 h/day, Monday to Saturday, Sunday off.**

Five weeks, 75 hours. No public holidays land on a study day this month — the first clean five-week run since October. Week 1 finishes standing up the lab from [June](2027-06.md) and freezes it; week 2 runs the intrusion chain and captures its telemetry into both engines; week 3 detects it in KQL/Sigma; week 4 detects the same chain a second time, natively in Wazuh; week 5 polishes and publishes the flagship writeup.

## Sources in use this month

- The lab from June: the Windows VM (Sysmon since December) as a Wazuh agent, telemetry also flowing to a free Azure Data Explorer cluster; a minimal DC or the January datasets for the domain links
- [Atomic Red Team](https://github.com/redcanaryco/atomic-red-team) — per-technique attack runners; *(optional)* [MITRE Caldera](https://github.com/mitre/caldera) to chain them into one operation
- Your KQL (from the ADX cluster), the [Sigma](https://github.com/SigmaHQ/sigma) specification, and your [Wazuh](https://wazuh.com/) instance running since February
- [MITRE ATT&CK](https://attack.mitre.org/) — every attack and every detection is tagged to a technique ID

> Run the real technique procedures and detect what they **actually** emit. The point is not to hand-write a log line and then match it — it is to see what Sysmon and Windows Security really record when the technique runs, and write the rule against that. A technique that produces no usable telemetry on this host is a real, publishable finding (a detection gap), not a failure to hide.

## Rules for a week that goes wrong

- **The lab freezes on schedule in week 1.** Week 2 cannot attack what week 1 is still plumbing. If stand-up runs long, cut the chain to fewer techniques, never the freeze.
- **Run real procedures; detect real telemetry.** Do not fabricate a log entry and match it. If a technique emits nothing useful on this host, that is a detection gap to write up, not to paper over.
- **A detection that isn't tuned against the baseline isn't a detection.** "It fired on the attack" is half a rule; "and stayed quiet on normal activity" is the other half.
- **Week 4 is not optional padding.** The second engine is the direct answer to "this plan is all Microsoft tools" — do not let it slip because week 3 already produced a working detection.
- **The detections are the deliverable, not the attack.** Anyone can run an atomic; the junior a SOC wants is the one who writes the rule that catches it — in more than one engine.
