# July 2027 — Stage 2, Purple-Team Project: Attack → Detect Twice Over

Month 10 of [the plan](../README.md). **15 h/week: 2.5 h/day, Monday to Saturday, Sunday off.**

Five weeks, 75 hours. No public holidays land on a study day this month — the first clean five-week run since October. Week 1 finishes instrumenting the app started in [June](2027-06.md) and freezes it; weeks 2–3 attack and detect in KQL/Sigma the way the plan always intended; week 4 is new — the same captured attack, detected a second time, natively in Wazuh; week 5 polishes and publishes the flagship writeup.

## Sources in use this month

- The instrumented target app from June — a starter or known-vulnerable app you stood up and logged, running locally
- Sysmon (installed since December)
- Your KQL skills (from the demo environment) and the [Sigma](https://github.com/SigmaHQ/sigma) specification
- Your [Wazuh](https://wazuh.com/) instance, running since February — this month it earns its keep

> Instrument the application the way you would if you were defending it in production — and log like someone who will have to detect the attack, twice, in two different engines. A planted bug teaches nothing; a real weakness in the starter app that survives to week 2 is the whole deliverable.

## Rules for a week that goes wrong

- **The app has to freeze on schedule in week 1.** Week 2 cannot attack what week 1 is still instrumenting. If the instrumentation runs long, cut scope, never the freeze.
- **Do not plant bugs.** The writeup is honest or it is worthless — and finding none is a real result to write up too.
- **Week 4 is not optional padding.** It is the direct answer to "this plan is all Microsoft tools" — do not let it slip just because week 3 already produced a working detection.
- **The detections are the deliverable, not the bugs.** Anyone can break a web app; the junior a SOC wants is the one who can write the rule that catches it — in more than one engine.
