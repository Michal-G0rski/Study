# July 2027 — virtual memory, and the heap as a data structure

Month 10 of [the plan](../README.md). **15 h/week: 2.5 h/day, Monday to Saturday, Sunday off.**

Scope only — what to read, what to build, what has to exist by Sunday. Setup and tooling are deliberately not here. Standing rules are in [plans/README.md](README.md).

Four clean weeks, sixty hours, no holidays.

The [checkpoint](../README.md#employability-checkpoint--months-910) runs through this month and closes at the end of it — not because applications stop, but because from August they stop being a scheduled block and become something you do when a posting appears.

On the main track: virtual memory, then the **Malloc Lab**. After this month the heap stops being the place memory comes from and becomes a data structure with an internal layout you can draw — which is the entire precondition for the heap exploitation in Stage 4.

## Sources in use this month

- [Computer Systems: A Programmer's Perspective](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~213/) — chapter 6 finished, then chapter 9, and the **Malloc Lab** from the [self-study labs](http://csapp.cs.cmu.edu/3e/labs.html).
- [picoCTF / CyLab Security Academy](https://cylabacademy.org) — continuing, and the [first CTF writeup](../README.md#portfolio-and-visibility) comes out of it this month.
- The [checkpoint](../README.md#employability-checkpoint--months-910), at five hours a week in weeks 1 and 3.

> On the Cache Lab: it is not scheduled. It is a performance lab — write a cache simulator, then optimise a matrix transpose — and the optimisation half is the part of CS:APP this path needs least. The **reading** for it, sections 6.4 to 6.7, is scheduled in full, because cache behaviour is the mechanism behind the side-channel work in [Stage 5](../README.md#stage-5--embedded-and-hardware) and behind a whole family of microarchitectural attacks. You want to understand a cache miss. You do not need to have simulated one.

## Rules for a week that goes wrong

The [standing rules](README.md) apply. Three things specific to this month:

- **The Malloc Lab is the last CS:APP lab in this plan and the one Stage 4 uses most directly.** Dynamic Allocator Misuse is a whole pwn.college module; it assumes you have built the thing you are about to abuse.
- **Chapter 9 does not get skimmed.** Virtual memory is the subject that everything after it — ASLR, mapping, kernel exploitation, firmware rehosting — quietly assumes.
- **If an interview lands, it outranks the schedule.** That is the entire point of the checkpoint. A week lost to interview preparation is a week the plan was designed to spend.

## Where August picks up

Month 11. CS:APP closes — exceptional control flow, system-level I/O, concurrency — and then **[Stage 3](../README.md#stage-3--low-level-and-assembly) opens** with x86-64 assembly proper.

From August the plan changes shape. There is no more single large course to walk through; there are several tracks at once, and at least a third of the time is hands-on, as the main plan requires from Stage 3 onward.
